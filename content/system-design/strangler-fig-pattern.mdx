- **It takes years, and the business keeps moving.** A full rewrite can take years, and during that time the business still needs new features. Those features either have to be built twice, once in the old system and once in the new one, or the new system gradually falls behind.
- **Hidden rules.** The legacy system contains years of small rules and edge cases that may no longer be documented. Some are only discovered when the new system behaves differently.
- **One big switchover.** Eventually, everything depends on one large switchover. If something goes wrong, every user can be affected at once.

This is why large rewrites are often much harder than they first appear.

<h1 id="2-where-the-name-comes-from">2. Where the Name Comes From</h1>

The Strangler Fig pattern takes a different approach. Martin Fowler named it in 2004 after strangler fig trees he saw in the rainforests of Queensland, Australia.

A strangler fig begins as a seed high in the branches of another tree. It sends roots down toward the ground and gradually grows around the host. Over time, the fig becomes larger while the original tree declines. Eventually, the host dies and the fig stands on its own in roughly the same place.

There is no point where the old tree is suddenly removed and replaced.

That is the idea behind the pattern. The new system gradually grows around the old one, taking over one piece at a time until the legacy system is no longer needed.
